# KCI (Key Customer Issue) Playbook

## What is a KCI?

The Key Customer Issue mechanism provides visibility to AWS leadership about issues that have a negative impact for key customers. KCIs are presented at the **AWS Weekly Business Review (WBR)** — Andy Jassy's meeting. Each KCI is a one-page narrative written to align all teams.

## KCI Tenets

1. **Awareness and Visibility** — We actively support and champion customer needs at the highest levels of the organization. KCI alerts AWS S-Team leaders to customer issues.
2. **Customer Advocacy** — We are customer obsessed. Issues for any customer, regardless of size and spend, can be taken to KCI.
3. **Reoccurrence Prevention** — We fix the problem right the first time for this customer AND other customers, so it does not happen again.
4. **Critical Prioritization** — Though all customer concerns matter, KCI prioritizes issues with threat of financial or reputational damage, productivity loss, or executive-level visibility.
5. **Cause Indifferent** — KCI triggers with sharp decline of customer sentiment, irrespective of cause — customer issue(s), AWS issue(s), or service event(s).
6. **Sentiment Repair** — KCI goes beyond technical fixes to repair the customer relationship and restore trust.

## When Does a KCR Become a KCI?

A KCR advances to KCI when:
- Temperature matrix shows RED/RED, RED/YELLOW, or YELLOW/RED
- Issue has executive visibility or media/reputational risk
- Support leadership review determines WBR visibility is needed
- Customer trust severely impacted regardless of technical progress

**Important**: Nearly all KCIs presented at the AWS WBR have a fully developed Get-to-Green plan already being implemented. Being on path to resolution does NOT prevent KCI.

## KCI Document Structure

The KCI is a one-page narrative containing:

1. **Customer Context** — Who is the customer, what they do on AWS, relationship significance
2. **Issue Summary** — What happened, when it started, customer impact
3. **Operational Impact** — Technical details, scope, services affected
4. **Business Sentiment** — Customer's current state of mind, churn risk, executive engagement
5. **Get-to-Green Plan** — Actions being taken:
   - Technical resolution steps
   - Customer relationship repair
   - Timeline and owners
   - Prevention measures
6. **Current Status** — Where we are today
7. **Ask** — What help is needed from leadership/S-Team

## KCI Writing Best Practices

- Write for S-Team audience — assume they don't know the technical details
- Lead with customer impact, not AWS internal process
- Be honest about root cause even if it's embarrassing
- Include clear timeline of events
- Show both technical fix AND sentiment repair in Get-to-Green
- Quantify impact where possible (revenue, hours of downtime, users affected)
- Don't minimize — if it's at KCI level, it's serious

## KCI Lifecycle

```
KCR Created → KCR-Focus → KCI Nomination → KCI Review → WBR Presentation → Active Tracking → Resolution → Closure
```

## Manager Actions for KCI

### When Your Team's Case Becomes a KCI

1. **Acknowledge urgency** — This has S-Team visibility now
2. **Assign dedicated owner** — Engineer with bandwidth for sustained engagement
3. **Clear blockers** — Deprioritize other work for assigned engineer
4. **Update cadence** — Hourly for RED, every 4hr for YELLOW
5. **Engage service teams** — Ensure all dependent teams are engaged
6. **Communication** — Keep ESM and account team updated on all progress
7. **Document everything** — All actions, decisions, and communications

### Preventing KCIs

- Monitor KCR-Lites and KCRs early — intervene before they escalate
- Address "paper cuts" proactively
- Track customer sentiment in 1:1s with TAMs
- Review aging escalations weekly
- Ensure Get-to-Green plans have realistic timelines
